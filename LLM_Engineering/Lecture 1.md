## 🚀 2. What Are Frontier Models?

### 🔹 Definition

- **Frontier Models** = The most advanced AI models available today.
    
- Built by large companies known as **Frontier Labs**.
    

### 🔹 Key Characteristics

- Extremely powerful
    
- Expensive to train (often **hundreds of millions of dollars**)
    
- Expensive to run (require massive computing resources)
    

### 🔹 Important Note

- “Frontier” is an **informal term**:
    
    - No strict definition
        
    - Depends on **context and usage**
        

---

## 🔒 3. Closed Source Models

### 🔹 What Are They?

- Models that:
    
    - Require **payment or API access**
        
    - Are **not publicly accessible**
        
- Owned and controlled by companies
    

### 🔹 Why Closed Source?

- High training cost
    
- Ongoing infrastructure costs
    
- Companies need to **recover investment**
    

### 🔹 Alternate Names

- Frontier Models
    
- Closed Source Frontier Models
    

---

## 🌍 4. Open Source Models

### 🔹 What Are They?

- Models that:
    
    - Can be **downloaded and run locally**
        
    - Are more accessible to developers
        

### 🔹 Example

- Meta created **LLaMA models**
    

### 🔹 “Open Source” vs “Open Weights”

- Some argue these models are:
    
    - **Open Weights**, not fully open source
        
- Reason:
    
    - Weights are available
        
    - Training data & methods are often **not disclosed**
        

---

## ⚖️ 5. Frontier vs Open Source

| Feature       | Closed Source (Frontier) | Open Source       |
| ------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------- |
| Access        | Paid / restricted        | Freely available  |
| Performance   | Usually higher           | Improving rapidly |
| Transparency  | Low                      | Moderate          |
| Customization | Limited                  | High              |

## 🏢 6. Major Frontier Model Providers (The “Big Four”)

---

### 🤖 6.1 OpenAI — GPT Models

- Most famous AI lab and model family
    

#### Key Points:

- **ChatGPT (GPT-3.5)** launched ~Nov 2022
    
- Triggered widespread adoption of AI
    
- Current version: **GPT-5** (as per lecture)
    
- Previously had:
    
    - Separate “O-series” models
        
- Now:
    
    - **Unified into GPT-5**
        

---

### 🧪 6.2 Anthropic — Claude Models

#### Background:

- Founded by former OpenAI employees
    

#### Model Variants:

- **Haiku** → Small, fast
    
- **Sonnet** → Balanced (currently strongest)
    
- **Opus** → Large, powerful
    

#### Current Version:

- **Claude 4.6 Sonnet**
    

#### Highlights:

- Highly favored by developers
    
- Strong in reasoning and coding
    
- Powers tools like Claude Code
    

---

### 🔍 6.3 Google — Gemini Models

#### Evolution:

- Started with **Bard** (poor reception)
    
- Rapid improvement → now competitive
    

#### Current Version:

- **Gemini 2.5 Pro**
    
- **Gemini 3** expected soon
    

#### Advantages:

- Free or discounted access for students (region-dependent)
    
- Strong multimodal capabilities
    

---

### ⚡ 6.4 xAI — Grok

#### Key Points:

- Considered part of the **“Big Four”**

## 🧩 8. Key Takeaways

- **Frontier Models** = Most advanced AI systems (usually closed source)
    
- **Open Source Models** = More accessible but sometimes less powerful
    
- The **Big Four companies dominate the space**:
    
    - OpenAI (GPT)
        
    - Anthropic (Claude)
        
    - Google (Gemini)
        
    - xAI (Grok)
        
- Choosing the right model depends on:
    
    - Task requirements
        
    - Cost
        
    - Performance needs (covered later in the course)
